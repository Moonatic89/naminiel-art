At the Edge of Time and Chaos

Tempora stood at the heart of her castle, an immense structure that seemed raised from the fabric of time itself. Every hall shifted in color like the hours of the day, from dawn gold to night blue, through purple and silver.

The queen of time watched the great hall from the top of her staircase. Her stern gaze seemed carved from stillness, wide enough to hold the whole universe at once.

From the garden below, the song of the White Ladies rose like the heartbeat of reality. Tempora closed her eyes for one brief moment, letting calm reach her. Then duty called, as it always did.

She crossed the suspended bridge between the castle towers, each one bound to an age of the world. Her children waited there, scattered like shadows and sparks through her realm.

"Tuesday," she called, her voice deep and steady. "Have you checked the flow of the day? Any disturbances?"

"None, Mother. But there was a strange deviation in human dreams. I wondered if—"

Tempora raised a firm hand. "Do not wonder. Observe. Analyse. Then report. Do not let curiosity distract you."

Farther on, Wednesday and Saturday were arguing. With a single gesture, she sent them back to their tasks. Order had to be maintained.

Then a familiar, chaotic presence struck her: Friday, running toward her with all the grace of a storm.

"Mother, mother, mother! Look what I found in the gardens!"

Friday lifted a feather from a Lumira, its soft glow trembling like twilight.

"It is a feather of chaos," Tempora said. "It is not a toy."

"But it is beautiful! Maybe I can make an amulet, or a secret weapon, or decorate my room!"

Tempora did not move. "Do what you wish. But remember: everything has weight in time."

Friday bounded away, and Tempora was left with a sigh. Her children carried her burden lightly because they did not yet know how heavy it was.

Later, in the garden of eternal twilight, she found Primula sitting among the Lumira. The White Ladies sang, and the universe seemed briefly balanced.

"Primula," Tempora whispered.

"You are here," Primula answered with a smile.

Tempora touched her wife's face. "The garden is perfect. Without you, it would not exist."

Primula's hand over hers softened the shadows in Tempora's heart. But after she left the garden, the queen climbed toward the highest tower, where an enormous suspended hourglass glowed above an empty throne.

"Always there, unmoving," she murmured. "A body that does not live, and a power that belongs neither to time nor to life."

She turned away sharply. "It is not mine to decide. Not now." Even in the castle's perfection, there were shadows the queen of time could not ignore.
