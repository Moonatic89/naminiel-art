Her Pillow

They stopped in a clearing wrapped in the soft light of twilight. The wind carried the damp scent of earth, and the rustle of leaves sounded like a distant song.

Friday dragged herself a few more steps, then dropped to her knees, exhausted. "I cannot go on..." she whispered, her voice barely more than breath.

Kumirai remained beside her, still as a mountain, his dark blue fur stirred by the breeze. As always, he did not answer.

"Please, say something," she insisted, letting her face sink into the softness of the bear's fur. "Even a sound, a sigh... anything that tells me you understand."

Kumirai gave a low, deep rumble, less like an answer than the breath of the earth itself. Then he slowly lowered himself beside her until he became a shelter.

"You are a terrible conversationalist, you know?"

The bear huffed softly, a small cloud in the cold evening air.

"Yes, yes, I know... but at least you stay."

And Kumirai stayed. Friday's breathing slowed as sleep wrapped her in the bear's warmth. She was the cub; he was her pillow.

Under the starlit sky, the girl and her silent guardian surrendered to the quiet of the night.
