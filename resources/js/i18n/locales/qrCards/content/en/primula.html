The Dancer of Silence

Hidden among the branches of a garden wrapped in twilight, Lauro saw for the first time a figure who seemed to embody silence itself. Primula moved with an ethereal grace, her steps as light as the wind over flower petals.

Around her, the Lumira circled like luminous shadows. Their winged bodies danced in the dim evening glow, weaving an ancient pattern too mysterious for mortal eyes.

From the castle of Tempora, watching above the garden, came a distant echo of stone and time. Its towers seemed to study the horizon like silent guardians, and Lauro felt suddenly small: an intruder among secrets older than his life.

Primula seemed untouched by that vigilance. Around her, the White Ladies moved in a circle, singing melodies thin and persistent as cicadas, until their voices became part of the air.

Lauro could not look away. She felt fragile and radiant, a meeting point between the garden and eternity. Yet beneath the sweetness of her face lay something fathomless, a sorrow without voice that slipped into the heart like a forgotten memory.

He did not know whether the garden was welcoming him or warning him. He only knew that this place, this hour, and that silent dancer would leave a mark no passing season could erase.
