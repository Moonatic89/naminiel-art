Primora - The Feast of the First Breath

Of all the feasts celebrated by the people of Vestara, none is as ancient, or as intimately tied to every creature's fate, as Primora, the Feast of the First Breath.

It is said to have begun when Lauro, still a young spirit of life, saw leaves fall for the first time. He watched the trees grow bare and believed it was a final ending.

Primula taught him otherwise. She took a fallen leaf, crushed it to dust between her fingers, and breathed it into the wind, saying that every cycle carries a new breath. From that simple, loving gesture, Primora was born.

Over the centuries, the feast became a pillar of the Vestarian year. No one willingly misses it, because it belongs to every life, every birth, and every loss.

When a child is born, an Breath Tree is planted for them. As Primora approaches, each person returns to their tree the way one returns to an old friend: quietly, without demands, carrying only what the heart has gathered through the year.

On that day, the clearings fill with color. Trees are decorated with ribbons, amber leaves, hand-carved charms, and thoughts written on bark or parchment.

It is a way of saying: if something must end, let it be surrounded by beauty and love.

Each tree keeps one final gift: the last autumn leaf. It must fall on its own. No one may tear it away, and no one dares touch it before its time.

When the wind releases it, the leaf is gathered with both hands like a fragile heart, dried, and ground into precious Lauro Powder.

With it, people prepare Donodoro, a soft spiral cake that recalls the motion of the wind, along with stews, sweets, and warm drinks.

During the great celebrations, no one is left without a slice. Homes open, strangers become guests, and on that day there is no distance between hearts.

As Donodoro is broken, every Vestarian speaks the blessing by memory:

"May the last autumn leaf never fall."

They say it to friends, children, lovers, and travelling companions. It is both a wish and a promise: may their ending not arrive before its time, and when it comes, may they not be alone.

Couples often add a quieter vow: "And if it falls, may it fall into my hands."

For those who live far away, the bond continues through a Twin Branch. For those who have lost their tree, a small shrub called Lauro's Memory becomes a bridge.

Primora is not the end of autumn, but the first breath of the new year.

Because, as an old saying of the Embrace goes:
"No ending remains an ending when it passes through loving hands."
